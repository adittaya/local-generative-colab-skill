Read this before comparing rows — caveats & how to read the chart
These are not apples-to-apples. Each row comes from a different benchmark with its own resolution, frame count, step count, sampler and quantisation. A 7-minute T4 row and a 26-minute T4 row may be producing very different amounts of video. Compare within a group, not across groups.
  • Resolution and frames move the number more than the model. Dropping from 720p to 480p, or from 81 to 49 frames, can cut render time by more than half.
  • Step count is the other big lever. Distilled checkpoints (4–8 steps) finish several times faster than 30–50-step dev checkpoints, at a small fidelity cost.
  • Quantisation buys VRAM, not speed. On GGUF builds the dequantisation step can itself become the bottleneck — which is why extra attention speed-ups don't always help.
  • The T4 is compute-limited, not just VRAM-limited. It is Turing (sm75): no native FP8 or BF16, and roughly a quarter of a 4090's memory bandwidth. That is why the same model is minutes on a T4 and seconds on a 4090.
  • On Colab the real ceiling is often host RAM (~12.7 GB), not VRAM (~15 GB). That is why MiniMax H3 needs a three-stage encode → sample → decode split to fit.
  • OOM means the configuration ran out of memory at that resolution — it is not a model failure, just a hardware limit.
